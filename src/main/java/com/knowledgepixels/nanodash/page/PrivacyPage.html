Privacy

Nanodash is open-source software that anyone can run, so what follows describes what the software does with your personal data, and who runs this particular instance.

Who is responsible

The operator of this instance.

This instance has not been configured to name its operator. Whoever gave you its address runs it, and your questions about your data go to them.

Logging in

Logging in through ORCID tells this instance your ORCID iD and the name on your ORCID record, which are kept on the server so that what you publish can be attributed to you.

Your signing key

Nanodash generates a signing key for you and keeps it on the server, so that what you publish carries your signature. An operator may also hold encrypted backups of these keys.

What you publish

Publishing is public and permanent. A nanopublication you publish carries your ORCID iD and your signature, and is distributed to a decentralised network of servers beyond this instance's control. It cannot afterwards be edited, withdrawn or deleted, by you or by us. Do not publish anything you may want to take back. Nanopublications you mark as protected are the exception: those stay on this instance.

Cookies and logs

A session cookie keeps you logged in. It is needed for the site to work and is not used to follow you anywhere else. Nanodash itself does not record your IP address. The web server an instance runs behind usually keeps short-lived access logs of the requests it serves, as web servers generally do.

This instance counts page views with Umami, which sets no cookies and does not track you across websites.

Your rights

You can ask the operator named above for a copy of your data, or for its correction or deletion — except for nanopublications already published, which, as above, nobody can delete.
